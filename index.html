About

I'm a high school senior who builds software products and runs them, which is a different job than writing code.

Full-stack means I write the interface, the API, and the schema. But the part I keep coming back to is everything after that: what to charge, who it's actually for, and why the person who signed up on Tuesday never opened it again. Every project on this page taught me some version of that lesson, usually the expensive way.

My bias is toward fewer moving parts and getting something in front of a real person early. A lot of what I build has no build step at all — you open the file and it works — because the things that survive are the ones someone else can pick up without a manual. I'd rather have three finished products than ten repositories.

ReEnvision is the one I care most about. Turning 96 lessons into something a teacher can actually run, on a phone, with no connection, turned out to be the real work; the code was the cheap part. That gap — between a thing that technically functions and a thing people can use — is most of what I want to keep studying, and most of why I want to start companies.
