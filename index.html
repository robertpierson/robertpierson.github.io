About

I'm a high school senior who builds software and runs the organizations around it, which is a different job than writing code.

Full-stack means I write the interface, the logic, and the data layer. But the part I keep coming back to is everything after that: who it's actually for, how someone finds it, and why the person who showed up on Tuesday never came back. Both things I run taught me some version of that, usually the expensive way.

My bias is toward fewer moving parts and getting something in front of a real person early. Most of what I build has no build step at all — you open the file and it works — because the things that survive are the ones someone else can pick up without a manual. I'd rather finish two things properly than ship ten repositories.

ReEnvision is the one I care most about. Turning 96 lessons into something a teacher can actually run, on a phone, with no connection, turned out to be the real work; the code was the cheap part. Building the Big Things site taught me the same lesson one level up — good community software mostly fails from never being seen, not from being badly built. That gap, between a thing that works and a thing people actually reach, is what I want to keep studying and why I want to start companies.
